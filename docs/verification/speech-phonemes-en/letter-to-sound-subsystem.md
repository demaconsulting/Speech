## LetterToSoundSubsystem Verification

### Verification Approach

The SpeechPhonemesEn LetterToSoundSubsystem is verified by deterministic tests in
`test/DemaConsulting.Speech.Phonemes.En.Tests/LetterToSoundSubsystemTests.cs` with the real embedded model and the real
lexicon (for the words that are known not to be in it); nothing is mocked. The subsystem-level tests
(`LetterToSoundSubsystem_*`) check the shipped model as a whole: a golden corpus of about a hundred words that the
dictionary lacks, pinned to the model's output; the guarantee that no spelling is silent over a large sample; and
stability under concurrent use. The units are verified by their own `LtsModel_*` and `LetterToSound_*` tests in the same
file (see the unit verification documents).

The golden is a regression baseline of the trained model, not an oracle: its expected column is regenerated by the tools
command `regenerate-goldens --oov` and reviewed; a doubtful pronunciation is listed in
`docs/evaluation/phonemes-en/oov.md` and never corrected by hand. The accuracy of the model on held-out dictionary words
is the result of the tools command `benchmark-lts`, recorded in that document, and the identity of the shipped model
with the one the training produces is verified by `train-lts --check`; both are build-level evidence and not part of the
package tests.

### Test Environment

- **Framework**: xUnit v3 running under the .NET SDK, on every target framework of the library
- **Execution**: `dotnet test` invoked by `build.ps1` and the CI pipeline
- **Project**: `test/DemaConsulting.Speech.Phonemes.En.Tests`
- **Files**: the golden file `TestData/oov-golden.tsv`, copied next to the test assembly
- **Isolation**: no network, no model file; the shared model and converter are immutable apart from the cache, whose
  answers do not depend on its state

### Acceptance Criteria

The subsystem tests pass when: every word of the golden corpus (at least 100 distinct words, none in the dictionary)
gets exactly its pinned pronunciation; a sample of more than 5,000 spellings (dictionary words, the golden words and
hostile spellings such as `www`, `sq`, `hmm`, apostrophes only around a letter and a 45-letter word) has a non-empty
pronunciation with a vowel; and 64 parallel loops converting the golden words give the single-threaded answers.

### Test Scenarios

#### Pinned out-of-vocabulary pronunciations

**Test**: `LetterToSoundSubsystem_OovGolden_EveryWordGetsItsPinnedPronunciation`

Names, brands, neologisms and compounds are converted and compared with the golden; the same test proves that none of
the golden words is in the dictionary, so the corpus really exercises the model.

#### Never silent

**Test**: `LetterToSoundSubsystem_AnySpelling_IsNeverSilent`

Every 25th dictionary word, the golden words and a set of hostile spellings are converted; none is silent and each has a
vowel.

#### Concurrent use

**Test**: `LetterToSoundSubsystem_ConcurrentUse_GivesTheSingleThreadedAnswers`

The golden words are converted from 64 parallel loops, including the first use of the shared instance and its cache;
every answer equals the single-threaded one.
